# Where the attention sink lives in modern vision encoders

Overnight run log, 2026-11-03. Follows [outlier-tokens-report.md](outlier-tokens-report.md) (claim A). All runs on Modal (T4 unless noted A10G), frozen timm checkpoints at native resolution, Imagenette/Imagewoof/ADE20K images, single pass (no seeds). Chart: [figures/sink-attention-by-depth.html](figures/sink-attention-by-depth.html) (data: [data/sink-attention.json](data/sink-attention.json)).

## The one-paragraph version

Almost every encoder family I measured has an attention sink: one token (or a few) that takes 30-65% of patch attention in the mid-to-late blocks, driven by a few fixed "massive" channels (top-5 channels hold 80-98% of its squared norm, as in LLMs). The exception is CLIP trained on open data (LAION, DFN, MetaCLIP). There the sink is weak and fades with scale, and at H/G size it is gone. What changes between encoders is **where the sink lives**, and that follows the training recipe and scale:

1. **Content patches** (CLIP OpenAI B/L, DINOv2 B/L/g, SigLIP 2 B/L, OpenCLIP L LAION, PE-Core G). Positions move with image content and are fragile: invisible noise relocates a quarter of them.
2. **One hard-coded patch** (AIMv2 at every size, SigLIP v1 and v2 So400m, SigLIP 2 g, and the PaliGemma 1/2 vision towers). The sink sits at the same patch in every image and is written by **one learned position embedding**. Swapping that embedding with another position's moves the sink there in 100% of images.
3. **A prefix token** (register in DINOv2+reg and DINOv3, CLS in PE-Core B/L). The sink token is nearly identical in every image.

Sinks are **conserved**. Delete them and the model makes new ones (DINOv2, AIMv2) or moves the sink to CLS. Removing a positional sink costs AIMv2 7.4 points of probe accuracy. Patch-outlier sinks store image-level information and lose local content. A training-free fix built for DINOv2 (test-time registers) transfers to CLIP and SigLIP 2 L but not AIMv2, and buys nothing on dense prediction.

## Findings by question

![Attention sink strength by depth](figures/sink-attention-by-depth.html "Mass on the most-attended key (default view) or on all high-norm patches (dropdown) per relative depth, for 12 encoders. Dashed = fixed-patch sinks, dotted = prefix sinks. 200-300 Imagenette val images, single pass.")

**G, sinks (run `9aa6e442`).** Patch outliers are attention sinks: CLIP's ~2.5 outliers per image take 37% of patch attention at block 16, DINOv2's single outlier takes 42% at block 23, SigLIP 2's ~2.3 take 50% at block 16. In clean encoders a prefix token does the same job (DINOv2+reg4 register 46%, DINOv3 register 40%, PE-Core CLS 64%). AIMv2's (0,0) patch is a sink from block 0 (43%), and graying that patch or rolling the image by 3 patches changes nothing.

**G2 and K2, mechanism (runs `8048b620`, `c53559d4`).** Swapping the sink's position embedding with (8,8) moves the top-norm token and its attention mass to (8,8) in 100% of images at every block, for AIMv2, SigLIP 2 So400m and SigLIP 2 g. Mass is preserved (SigLIP 2 So400m 0.447 → 0.445). Replacing AIMv2's (0,0) embedding with the mean embedding keeps the token high-norm but removes most of the sink (attention 0.43 → 0.002 at block 0). In all 7 ViT-L encoders the sink's top channel is the same channel in 100% of images. Its size relative to a typical patch channel: AIMv2 15,500x, CLIP 1,300x, SigLIP 2 1,200x, PE CLS 1,100x, DINOv2 700x, DINOv2+reg4 register 226x and DINOv3 register 23,400x (the last two from H, after fixing an indexing bug in G2).

**H, conservation and function (run `c33ffef2`).** Mean-ablating DINOv2's patch outliers at every block makes it create more (peak 3.0 → 4.8 per image) and moves the sink to CLS (top-key mass 0.47 → 0.13). Killing AIMv2's positional sink doubles its content outliers (16 → 34 per image) and drops Imagenette probe accuracy 99.0 → 91.6. Removing patch outliers costs CLIP, DINOv2 and SigLIP 2 at most 0.6 points. Mean-ablating a prefix sink is a no-op (DINOv3 0.397 → 0.397), because the sink token is input-invariant. Removing one would need an out-of-distribution ablation, the confound raised by [Zero-Ablation Overstates Register Content Dependence](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.14433).

**C and C2, what sinks store (runs `f91d2724`, `6afabb98`).** A linear probe on the outlier token beats a normal patch token by 17-24 points on Imagenette and 28-47 points on Imagewoof. Imagewoof scores: CLIP 69.5 vs 32.3, DINOv2 86.8 vs 39.4, SigLIP 2 84.7 vs 54.6, AIMv2's position-locked (0,0) sink 58.8 vs 31.2. In DINOv3 the sink register is the *least* informative register (Imagewoof 81% vs 96% for the others, last block).

**E and E2, does the DINOv2 fix transfer (runs `cb3305a8`, `2b7e7e71`).** Test-time registers (register-neuron shift onto one extra token) remove CLIP's outliers fully (K=20) and SigLIP 2 L's at K≥20. DINOv2 is non-monotonic in K. AIMv2's content outliers survive (16 → 11 per image). Appending the unused token by itself *raises* outliers in DINOv2 and AIMv2. Classification accuracy never moves. ADE20K linear segmentation moves within about 1 mIoU point (CLIP 18.6 → 17.7, DINOv2 31.5 → 31.9). Outlier patches are segmented badly (SigLIP 2 20% vs 61% accuracy) but are too few to matter.

**F, fragility (run `79a25608`, A10G).** Outlier count is stable under perturbation; positions are not. Gaussian noise with σ = 1/255 relocates 26% of CLIP's outliers, 22% of DINOv2's and 9% of SigLIP 2's; σ = 4/255 relocates 42-43% (CLIP, DINOv2). JPEG q75 relocates ≤2%. A horizontal flip keeps only 27-54% at the mirrored spot. AIMv2 is stable (98% kept at 1/255). A 10-step PGD could not create or remove outliers (≤8% / ≤1%), which says they are hard to steer by gradient, not that they are robust.

**J, resolution (run `adc30e2e`).** Clean encoders stay clean up to 2304 tokens. CLIP holds a fixed budget (2.5 → 2.8 outliers per image from 224 to 336). SigLIP 2 L (2.4 → 4.6 → 7.9) and AIMv2 (16 → 27 → 49) scale with token count.

**K4, removing the So400m sink (run `177ccfa0`).** SigLIP v1 So400m@384: the swap moves the sink (mass on the old patch 0.145 → 0.001, peak top-key mass unchanged) at no probe cost (Imagewoof 95.3 → 95.2). In v1 this sink is weaker (3-14% of attention) than in SigLIP 2 (30-45%). Killing it with the mean position embedding costs about 2 points in both (v1 95.3 → 93.5; SigLIP 2 So400m 95.2 → 93.2), and the sink comes back elsewhere at close to its old strength (SigLIP 2 top-key mass 0.39 vs 0.36 at block 21). In SigLIP 2 the new sink is not high-norm: patch outliers drop from 1.97 to 0.02 per image while attention still concentrates. Norm and sink can come apart.

**V, the sink inside a VLM (run `c7367092`).** LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B fine-tunes its SigLIP So400m tower during VLM training, and the sink survives: patch (21,2) is the top-norm token at all 26 blocks in 100% of images, the same patch as the original checkpoint. VisionZip-style attention ranking puts it at rank 1 in 100% of 1100 POPE/GQA items, so attention-based pruning always keeps it. Dropping that one index changes nothing measurable on single images: +0.0 points overall at k=64 (paired 95% CI −0.4 to +0.4), +0.2 at k=144. One wasted slot out of 64 doesn't show. SToP's gains from suppressing sinks were on video, where each frame brings its own sink and the wasted budget multiplies; that setting is untested here. My stand-in for SinkPruner's high-norm filter (4x median) never fired, because the sink sits under 4x at the feature layer, so I have no SinkPruner comparison. Caveats: single 384 view without anyres crops, and simplified VisionZip (no contextual-token merging), which was no better than random selection here.

**V2, drop after vs kill inside, and small budgets (run `7eb05244`).** Same LLaVA-OneVision setup, 1100 items, paired 95% CIs. Dropping the sink's output token while keeping all others costs nothing (+0.0 points, −0.3 to +0.3). Killing the sink inside the tower costs −1.4 (−2.5 to −0.2). So SToP is right that the output token is dispensable, and wrong that the sink has no structural function: the encoder needs it internally. Excluding the sink from attention-ranked top-k doesn't help reliably even at video-like budgets (k=4: −0.4, k=8: +0.3, k=16: +1.8 with CI touching 0, k=32: −0.3). A side result: attention-ranked top-k (simplified VisionZip, no merging) is far worse than random at small k, by 15.2 points at k=16 (CI 12.0 to 18.4). My guess is that attention ranking fills small budgets with several sink-like tokens, not just the fixed one. That fits SinkPruner's thesis, and it means a one-index fix can't be enough.

**K4-CI, how much killing a positional sink costs (run `2e951dfb`).** Three probe seeds, paired bootstrap over 1000 Imagewoof images. SigLIP 2 So400m: −1.93 points (−2.87 to −1.07), all three seeds agree. AIMv2 L: −28.5 points (−30.9 to −26.2), from 94.9 to 66.4. H's −7.4 on Imagenette understated it: on fine-grained data AIMv2 falls apart without its position-embedding sink.

**W, finding the sink from weights alone (run `5fe2568c`, no images).** For AIMv2 at every size, the sink's position-embedding row is a huge outlier (norm z ≈ 15.8 vs ≤ 2.4 for any other row). For SigLIP v1 So400m and the PaliGemma towers it is the top row with a thin margin. For SigLIP 2 So400m and g it fails: the sink row looks ordinary, though swapping it still moves the sink (K2). Models without a fixed sink also have outlier rows (SigLIP 2 L at (13,15), z = 12.6), and these match the weak early-block fixed tokens from claim A. So an outlier position-embedding row predicts an early fixed high-norm token, not reliably the late attention sink.

**A1, why AIMv2 collapses (run `bfdf36d2`).** Nothing rescues it once the sink is killed, and the damage is done inside the first block. Weakening the sink's position embedding by 25%, 50% or 75% toward the mean costs nothing (≤0.2 points); removing it fully costs 30 points. It is a cliff, not a slope. Putting the clean sink token back into the killed model after block 0, 1, 2, 4, 8 or 12 leaves accuracy at 66-68% (clean 95%, killed 65%). Restoring only its top-5 massive channels doesn't help either (64%). So the other patches are corrupted when they read the altered sink during block 0's attention, and fixing the sink afterwards is too late. The killed model's mean patch features become nearly unrelated to the clean model's (cosine ≈ 0.02 by block 11), and it rebuilds a weaker sink at the same spot by block 3 (23-70x the median vs 110-250x clean).

**A2, the sink works through its key and value (run `2e1807d8`).** The cliff is steep but continuous: blending the sink's position embedding 80% / 90% / 95% toward the mean costs 2.3 / 8.2 / 26.9 points. The decisive test leaves the sink token itself broken and gives the other patches the clean sink's key and value. In block 0 only, that recovers accuracy from 65.0% to 90.8%, 85% of the gap. At every block, it restores exactly 95.0%, identical to the clean model. Restoring only the sink's query does nothing (65.5%). The sink's whole job is to be an attention target the other patches read; its own hidden state doesn't matter. That's the LLM attention-sink mechanism, shown causally in a vision encoder, with the target hard-coded into one position embedding.

**A3, same mechanism in SigLIP 2 So400m (run `626e2a34`).** Killed: 93.4% vs 95.2% clean. Giving the other patches the clean sink's key and value in block 0 only, with the sink's own state still broken, restores 95.1%, a full rescue. At every block it restores 95.2% exactly. Restoring only the query does not help (92.8%). Two encoder families trained in different ways (autoregressive AIMv2, contrastive SigLIP 2) use their hard-coded sink the same way: as a key/value target that is read from the first block on.

**D, moving the sink off the grid at test time (run `2db55c09`).** Kill the position-embedding sink and append one constant token before block 0: the average clean input at the sink patch over 200 calibration images. No training. AIMv2 recovers fully (killed 65.0% → 94.9%, clean 95.0%). SigLIP 2 So400m recovers most of the way (93.4% → 94.6%, clean 95.2%). The constant token becomes the new sink (224x and 147x the median norm, matching the originals), and the image patch goes back to being ordinary in SigLIP 2 (2.5x). A zero token instead of the constant one gets AIMv2 to 93.3% but does nothing for SigLIP 2. In short, the hard-coded sink can be moved off the image grid without retraining.

**D-VLM, the same fix inside LLaVA-OneVision (run `3c6e8f4c`).** Only partial. Killing the tower's sink costs −1.4 points on POPE/GQA; adding the constant token brings that to −1.0 (−2.2 to +0.2), about 30% of the gap. The fine-tuned VLM tower does not take the substitute as cleanly as the bare encoders do.

**V3, replication on LLaVA-OneVision 7B (run `5365c0a1`).** Same sink at (21,2), ranked first by encoder attention in 100% of items. Dropping its output token: +0.1 points (0.0 to +0.4). Killing it inside the encoder: −1.2 (−2.5 to 0.0); killing plus dropping vs dropping alone: −1.3 (−2.5 to −0.2). Attention-ranked top-16 loses to random-16 by 19.5 points (16.4 to 22.7). Same direction and size as at 0.5B.

**VV, video QA (run `a66c6957`).** LLaVA-OneVision-0.5B on TempCompass (1580 multiple-choice questions, 16 frames). The pooled sink token sits at index 155, exactly the patch SToP reports. Removing it from every frame changes accuracy by +0.4 points (−0.3 to +1.1), and killing it inside the encoder by +0.0 (−0.9 to +0.9). Excluding it from per-frame top-k makes no difference (k=8: −0.1, k=16: +0.3), and random-k again beats attention top-k (+3.0 at k=8). I could not reproduce SToP's gain from suppressing sinks in this setup. That doesn't refute them: they score many sink tokens rather than one, use full pruning methods, and evaluate other benchmarks.

## What I think the paper is

**Working title:** *Hard-coded attention sinks in vision encoders.*

**Claim.** Most large vision encoders grow an attention sink, and the training recipe decides where it lives: content patches, a prefix token, or one hard-coded patch. The hard-coded kind (AIMv2, SigLIP So400m/g, and the PaliGemma and LLaVA-OneVision towers built on them) is written by a single learned position embedding. It works purely as a key/value target the other patches read from block 0 on: restoring only its key and value fully repairs a killed model. Its output token is dispensable in a VLM (dropping it costs nothing on images or video). The sink itself is not: removing it inside the encoder costs 1-2 points in SigLIP and 28 points in AIMv2 on fine-grained images. One constant extra token can take over its job at test time without retraining (full recovery in the bare encoders, partial in a fine-tuned VLM tower). Open-data CLIPs, which mostly lack a sink, are the counterexample a full account has to explain.

Why this is new, based on the papers I read:
- Register and outlier work stops at content-dependent patches or registers in DINO-family and OpenCLIP models ([Do All ViTs Need Registers?](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2603.25803), [Vision Transformers Need More Than Registers](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2602.22394), [Decoding Register and High-Norm Tokens](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.03698)).
- The fixed-position sink is a known LLM phenomenon. [Mind the Spike](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.32808) describes the text spike that "systematically appears in early layers at a fixed initial position, independently of input content" ([p. 1](https://www.alphaxiv.org/pdf/2609.32808?page=1)) and contrasts it with image-dependent visual spikes inside the LLM. Our result is that the *vision encoder itself* can have an input-independent, position-locked sink, with no causal mask needed: SigLIP is fully bidirectional.
- **Pruning work already found the symptom (checked 2026-11-03).** [SToP](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.20937) reports that in SigLIP-based video LLMs "high attention scores persist at fixed spatial coordinates", calls these sink tokens, and suppresses them during pruning. Its example indices 154/155 would land on our (21,2) sink if the 27x27 grid is pooled 2x to 14x14 (my inference, unchecked). [SinkPruner](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2609.01004) shows high-norm encoder tokens mislead attention-based pruning and filters them out. So "the sink hurts pruning, drop it" is taken. Neither has the mechanism (one position embedding, the swap moves it), the family-wide map (AIMv2, SigLIP So400m/g, PaliGemma, constant grid fraction), or the encoder-side cost. SToP argues encoder sinks have no structural function; killing the sink *inside* the encoder costs 2-7 probe points and the model rebuilds it (H, K4). Dropping the output token after the encoder and removing the sink inside it are different operations, and that distinction is ours.

## Open questions and limits

1. **Why the sink lands where it does.** Fixed sinks show up in AIMv2 at every size, but in SigLIP only at So400m and above. SigLIP 2's sink row doesn't even stand out by norm (W). Sorting out scale vs data vs architecture needs intermediate training checkpoints, which aren't public.
2. **The open-data CLIP exception.** LAION, DFN and MetaCLIP CLIPs have weak or no sinks, weakening with scale (MetaCLIP L → H → G: 0.18 → 0.09 → 0.045), while OpenAI CLIP has a strong one. The cause is open.
3. **Pruning claims.** I could not reproduce a benefit from suppressing the one fixed sink when pruning tokens, on images (V, V2, V3) or video (VV). Attention-ranked selection without token merging was consistently worse than random, which suggests several sink-like tokens dominate attention ranking. That's SinkPruner's territory, and I used a simplified VisionZip, not the real method.
4. **Rigor.** Most measurements are single-pass on 200-1600 items. Confidence intervals exist for the VLM results (paired bootstrap), the kill costs (3 seeds, K4-CI), and the mechanism tests (A1-A3, D); the core attention map was replicated on new images (G-rep). Linear probes on Imagenette sit near ceiling, so the key costs are on Imagewoof. The PGD attack in F was weak.
5. **Scope.** VLM tests use LLaVA-OneVision 0.5B/7B only (PaliGemma weights are gated), a single 384 view for images, and greedy decoding.
